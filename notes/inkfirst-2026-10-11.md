# Ink first, geometry second: prototype log (2026-10-11)

**Idea.** Find ink without a fitted mesh. Sample points on a published surface prediction, cut 66-layer patches along the local normal, ink them with the existing 9 µm surface models, and map the results back to 3D.

**Test block.** A 1.3 × 1.4 × 1.0 cm block of PHerc0139 around the densest known text of segment w047. The reference is the published 2.4 µm / 78 keV ink prediction.

## Results

- **Round 1** (recto prediction, stride 0.9 mm, Reader v2 and d9v4C)
  - On the 9–18 % of the w047 window covered, ink-first correlates with the known text at r 0.29–0.45, z +2.4 to +3.3 against 72 shifted references.
  - Our mesh-based pipeline on the same pixels: r 0.53–0.58.
- **Correction (same day).** d9v4C was trained on PHerc0139 w047. Every d9v4C result on this block is contaminated by training overlap:
  - round 2 (stride 0.6 mm, 50 % coverage, r 0.38);
  - the blended run (74 % coverage, r 0.32, z +6.3);
  - a first text-vs-no-text block comparison.

  The commit messages c3030e6..(this note) that quote those numbers should be read with this correction. Reader v2 held w047 out, so the Reader v2 part of round 1 stands: r 0.29–0.44, z +2.4 to +3.2.
- **Mesh-free sheet views** (`inkfirst_sheets.py`). At 1/4 resolution the block's sheets fuse into one connected component. Separating sheets is the segmentation problem itself.
