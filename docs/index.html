Vesuvius Challenge — PHerc0191 First Letters campaign

Research log: every run, what it asked, what it showed, what changed

Goal

Find 10 legible letters in a 4 cm² area of an unopened Herculaneum scroll (First Letters, $50k/scroll), and surface evidence-backed tooling fixes along the way (Progress Prizes).

Scroll

PHerc0191 — 9.362 µm / 113 keV scan 20250821151635, crushed cross-section, full public data (CT, Lasagna normals, tracks, surface predictions). Its clearest band is getting a last, deeper look; PHerc0813 is the next scroll (pilot).

Hardware

Home: 4-GPU Linux box (2× RTX 3060 12 GB, RTX 3060 Ti 8 GB; GTX 1660 SUPER unusable), i3-9100 4 cores, 1 TB NVMe, 300 Mbps WAN. This is the default. Cloud GPUs (AWS) only when it is cost-effective.

How the pipeline works

From an X-ray scan of a rolled-up scroll to a decision about where to look next, in nine steps. Concepts only; each run page has the details.

  1. 1CT scanGeometry

    An X-ray volume of the rolled scroll. Every slice is a cross-section through all of its wound layers.

    Scan data: Vesuvius Challenge / EduceLab

  2. 2Find the sheetGeometry

    Fit a spiral model of how the papyrus is wound to sheet tracks traced by the community and to predicted surfaces.

    villa spiral-fitting, Lasagna normals

  3. 3SnapGeometry

    Pull the fitted surface onto the papyrus surface a neural network predicted, so it sits on one real sheet.

    Erwin Nieuwlaar's snap, m7 surface prediction

  4. 4FlattenGeometry

    Unroll one winding into a flat sheet, so the papyrus, and any writing on it, appear at true size.

    Lasagna

  5. 5RenderInk

    Sample a thin stack of layers just inside and just outside the surface, like slicing the sheet into leaves.

    Volume Cartographer (villa)

  6. 6Detect inkInk

    Several public ink models each mark where ink might be. Their maps are combined into one ensemble, for the writing side and for the back.

    ink_9um, dense_native, Hecate; ensemble after Erwin Nieuwlaar

  7. 7RankInk

    The stroke score points to places where stroke-shaped marks show on the writing side but not on the back.

    stroke-score (this project)

  8. 8LookDecision

    We check every flag by eye against the CT and the back of the sheet. Cracks, voids and folds cause most false alarms.

  9. 9GateDecision

    Decide: go deeper here, move to the next band, or move to the next scroll. A new band or scroll starts again at step 2.

Positive controlruns alongside steps 5–8

Known letters from PHerc. 0343 (Erwin Nieuwlaar's First Letters segment) go through the same render, ink, rank and look steps. The letter rows have to light up there; if they did not, a blank result on our scroll would mean nothing. They do.

Findings

About the scroll and the geometry

  • The ink detector is not the problem

    Calibrated on known text: three legible Greek lines, and the PHerc. 343 control passes with the ensemble. The early PHerc0191 nulls were geometry; since the snap + flatten, the windings have clean papyrus weave, so the nulls after that are real nulls for the windings and depths checked.

  • Positive control passes only with the ensemble

    On the PHerc. 343 winning segment our four-model ensemble shows the letter rows; ink_9um seed 42 alone at whole-segment scale does not. Searches on 0191 need the ensemble (Hecate is the strongest single model).

  • Minimal-route spiral fits sit on the sheet ~46–49% of the time

    Satisfaction = within 6 voxels and 0.45 winding of the track's sheet. Weights don't help; 3× steps gives +3 points.

  • The fitted spiral never reaches the outer third

    Per-sector audit: outermost winding 3–8 mm short of the boundary in the fat sectors; initial spacing fixes the radial extent; stretching hurts the inner tracks.

  • Crushed scroll = same winding count, 2.5× spacing variation by sector

    0.13 mm on the flattened sides (ray audit under-counts there) to 0.26 mm at the ends. Needs anisotropic deformation or local spacing supervision.

  • Patch growing needs its normal grids

    Without normal_grid_path the tracer produces data-independent discs. With them it partly follows sheets.

  • The PHerc. 343 First Letters win (2026-10-08) used our pipeline plus three steps we lacked

    Spiral fit with the surface-prediction distance field, no shell, 30k steps; post-hoc snap of every vertex onto the nearest predicted sheet (±15 vox); 66-layer render swept over 31 depths through a four-model ink ensemble. All open source; adopted (see notes/prior-art-digest.md).

  • PHerc0191: no ink in any winding checked

    Band z 9,000–10,000: all 99 flattened windings at depths 0 and +1 (7 four-model, 92 three-model). The community's 10 windings. The seven best clear-band windings at depths −2..+3 with four models. No letters anywhere.

  • Our old band was a below-average part of the scan

    Scan-Quality-Map: 0.37–0.40 for z 9,000–10,000 against a scroll median of 0.41 and 0.54 for the clearest band, where our fit is also our best (0.519 on-sheet).

  • A crack band can look like a signal

    The strongest PHerc0191 scores (w073 and w074 at depth +2) came from soft blobs along a CT crack band, present at several depths and faintly on the blind side. Not writing.

  • Next scroll: PHerc0813

    Top three at 9.362 µm on two clarity measures, round cross-section, the longest clear stretch, every input published, same orientation as PHerc0191. Then PHerc0358, then PHerc0175A.

About the tools (Progress Prize material)

  • #1 libvolcomp not installed by the VC3D build (fixed, PR #2008)

    One-line CMake install fix; submitted.

  • #2–#5 image tags, lockfile rewrite, remote-fetch aborts

    :stable tag missing; uv.lock rewritten by sync; infer and renderer abort on a single transient remote chunk fetch.

  • #6 Shipped track-crossing cache rejected after download

    Signature uses nanosecond mtime; any download zeroes sub-second mtime. 2.4 GB downloaded for nothing; every run rebuilds.

  • #7 Outer-shell loss silently zero for sparse shells

    Confidence normalized by an edge spike → 0.22 < 0.25 → no valid samples, no warning. Reproduced outside the fitter.

  • #8 Fiber inference needs lasagna on PYTHONPATH

    geometry.py imports lasagna.omezarr_pyramid unguarded; the models extra does not ship it.

  • Upstream removed the inputs the winner used (Spiral simplification, 2026-09-23)

    surf-SDT support and the 'phase' dense-spacing mode were deleted from spiral-fitting three weeks before they won a prize. We run the pre-removal commit in a worktree to reproduce the route.

  • #9 Resident-pool sidecars reused after the source store is rebuilt

    The fitter silently trained on a stale SDT pool (1 of 1,456 bricks) after the store was rebuilt at the same path.

  • #10 Raw spiral windings render 23–33% compressed

    Meshes declare a 20-voxel grid but nodes are 26–30 voxels apart; ink models see letters too small unless the winding is flattened first. Mesh areas are understated ~2×.

  • stroke-score 0.1: triage for ink searches (MIT)

    Ranks windings, depths and 4 cm² windows by stroke-shaped ink on the reading side against the blind side, with review crops. Calibrated on the PHerc. 343 control and 109 null windings; known false alarms documented.

Question

How well does the minimal-route spiral fit (umbilicus + Lasagna + tracks) follow PHerc0191's sheets?

Technique

spiral-fitting fit_spiral.py, dense_spacing_mode grad_mag, tracks on, no patches/shell/winding model. 46 min on one RTX 3060.

Result

120 windings exported; on-sheet track points 46%, fully satisfied tracks 8%. Renders of w040/w070/w100 are on-sheet only in patches; ink maps are noise.

What we learned

A point counts as on-sheet only within 6 voxels and 0.45 winding of its track's sheet, so more than half the track points are off the fitted surface. Ink is noise until that changes.

Not yet. A4: a three-model ink check at depths 0 and +1 on the other 93 windings of the clear band, so a null means the whole band is null.
